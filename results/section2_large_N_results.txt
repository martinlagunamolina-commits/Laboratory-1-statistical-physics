SECTION 2k: LARGE-N LIMIT
N = 10^23 unbiased coins
Analytical approximations are used; no enumeration is performed.

a) Probability of exactly 30 heads
   log10(P(k=30)) = -30102999566398119520716.3131325242283964492208675752343530335
   P(k=30) is approximately 10 raised to this exponent.

b) Probability of the central macrostate
   P(k=N/2) approximately sqrt(2/(pi*N))
   P(k=N/2) = 2.523132522020E-12

c) Cumulative probability within +/- 10 heads
   21 central probabilities are practically equal.
   P(|k-N/2| <= 10) = 5.298578296242E-11

d) Cumulative probability within +/- 20 heads
   41 central probabilities are practically equal.
   P(|k-N/2| <= 20) = 1.034484334028E-10

e) Entropy difference for N/2 + 25 versus N/2
   Delta S approximately -2*k_B*25^2/N
   Delta S = -1.725811250000E-43 J/K

f) Entropy difference for N/2 + 10 versus N/2
   Delta S approximately -2*k_B*10^2/N
   Delta S = -2.761298000000E-44 J/K

g) Entropy difference between N/2 and 20 heads
   Delta S = S(N/2) - S(20)
   Delta S = 9.569929616929E-1 J/K

h) Expected waiting time for exactly 8 heads
   log10(P(k=8)) = -30102999566398119521194.4949929727401456955729732467588889257
   log10(expected waiting time in seconds) = 30102999566398119521194.4949929727401456955729732467588889257
   Thus the waiting time is approximately 10 raised to this exponent seconds.

i) Likelihood ratio: central macrostate / macrostate N/2 + 10
   log(ratio) approximately 2*10^2/N = 1.99999999999999999999997999999999999999999996814666666666667E-21
   Ratio approximately 1 + 2.000000000000E-21

j) Second Law interpretation
   The Second Law is statistical, not an absolute prohibition of entropy-decreasing
   fluctuations. A lower-entropy macrostate is possible, but it has fewer
   compatible microstates and therefore a much smaller probability. The central
   macrostate is overwhelmingly favoured over strongly ordered states such as
   k=20. For N=10^23, a fluctuation from the central region to such a state is
   extraordinarily unlikely, but it is not mathematically forbidden. Thus the
   results do not contradict the Second Law: they explain its statistical meaning.

Large-N conclusions
   Standard deviation: sqrt(N)/2 = 1.581138830084E+11 heads.
   Relative fluctuation: 1/sqrt(N) = 3.16227766017E-12.
   For fixed k, P(k) approximately N^k/(k!*2^N), which tends to zero
   exponentially as N increases. The central single-macrostate probability
   also tends to zero, approximately as N^(-1/2).
   A fixed window of +/-10 or +/-20 heads is negligible compared with the
   fluctuation width, which grows as sqrt(N).
   For a fixed offset m from N/2, Delta S is approximately -2*k_B*m^2/N
   and tends to zero. In contrast, S(N/2)-S(20) grows approximately as
   N*k_B*ln(2). The likelihood ratio for a fixed offset tends to 1.
